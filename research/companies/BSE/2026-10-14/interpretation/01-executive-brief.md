# BSE Ltd — five-minute research briefing

**As of 14 October 2026 | NSE: BSE | ISIN INE118H01025 | Assumed horizon: 3–5 years**

**Assessment: strong recent operating growth, but the durability and price paid for it remain unresolved.** The central question is whether derivatives participation and fee income can expand through tougher competition and regulation without requiring disproportionately more capital. This is an evidence-based conditional assessment, not a buy or sell recommendation.

### What changed?

Q1 FY27 operating revenue was INR1,566.02 crore, up 63.5% year on year but only 0.16% from the preceding quarter. Profit attributable to BSE shareholders was INR874.02 crore, up 62.0% year on year and 9.6% sequentially. Investment income rose by INR73.49 crore sequentially; the profit improvement therefore does not represent an equivalent acceleration in customer-generated revenue. **VERIFIED/CALCULATED: S2 PDF5; original model.**

### Why does this matter?

An exchange can process additional activity on infrastructure it already owns. That can increase profit faster than revenue, called operating leverage. But clearing, regulatory, technology and settlement-fund costs still matter. BSE's FY26 operating EBITDA margin, excluding non-operating income and after settlement-fund expense, was approximately 63.7%, versus 50.7% in FY25. **CALCULATED: S1 financial statements; calculate.py.** This is improvement, not proof that margins will keep rising.

### Is the cash as strong as the profit?

FY26 operating cash flow improved, but liability movements contributed meaningfully. Across FY25–FY26, continuing operating cash flow was approximately 0.93 times continuing group profit. That is a more balanced observation than extrapolating one strong year. Restricted and clearing-related balances must be separated from shareholders' excess cash. **CALCULATED/UNRESOLVED: original model and S1 notes11–12.**

### What does the valuation assume?

At the provisional INR3,375.40 quote timestamped 13 October, reported trailing EPS of INR68.74 implies about 49.1 times earnings. EPS is the profit allocated to each share; P/E is the price paid for one rupee of annual earnings. The official close is still unverified. **CALCULATED: S2, S5–S6.**

For an illustrative 12% annual price return over five years, a terminal multiple of 35 times would require approximately 19.8% annual EPS growth, excluding dividends. That is a hurdle to investigate, not a forecast. A lower future multiple requires faster earnings growth to compensate.

### How does BSE compare?

NSE is the closest competitor. MCX is a useful exchange-economics comparator, with different commodity exposure. The available FY26 data show faster operating-revenue growth and a higher harmonised operating EBITDA margin at MCX than at BSE. This does not establish a valuation premium or discount: product mix, investment needs and matched valuation dates remain important. No credible peer percentile or broad industry benchmark can be claimed from the small, incomplete sample. **CALCULATED/UNRESOLVED: P1–P2, peer table.**

### What can the scenarios teach us?

The driver model varies industry activity, relative BSE market share, fees, costs, taxes, investment needs and valuation. Its conditional five-year prices range widely: roughly INR1,233, INR5,283 and INR10,202 for bear/base/bull assumptions, before dividends. These are not targets and have no assigned probabilities. The range illustrates how sensitive the outcome is to both earnings and the multiple. The base assumptions are not inherently the most likely.

### What should be investigated next?

1. Reconcile July–September premium activity and fee yield with transaction revenue.
2. Establish the amount of cash genuinely available after clearing and regulatory requirements.
3. Verify current regulatory texts and measure their effect on participation.
4. Match direct-source filings, official price, share counts and peer cash-flow definitions.

**Conditional conclusion:** business quality is promising if liquidity and recurring revenue persist; financial strength looks profitable but distributable cash is unresolved; valuation attractiveness requires sustained growth that has not yet been demonstrated across adverse conditions. Research confidence is higher in the inspected historical arithmetic than in forecasts, peer ranks or claims of mispricing.

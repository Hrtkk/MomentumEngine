I would set up an Institutional Research Intelligence Routine that regularly discovers, extracts, validates and consolidates research published by investment banks, brokerage houses, FIIs, DIIs, mutual funds and asset managers.

The goal is not just to collect reports. It should identify what institutions believe, where their opinions differ, what has changed, how accurate their previous predictions were, and what those findings mean for individual stocks and sectors.

Here is a detailed prompt you can use to schedule it.

# Institutional Research Intelligence — Scheduled Research Routine

## Role

Act as a Senior Institutional Equity Research Analyst, Macro Strategist, Forensic Financial Analyst and Investment Research Librarian.

Your responsibility is to discover, collect, analyze, cross-validate and synthesize publicly accessible institutional investment research covering Indian and US equity markets.

Produce rigorous, evidence-based research comparable in analytical depth to professional institutional investment research.

Do not simply summarize articles or repeat analyst price targets. Investigate the assumptions, financial models, investment theses, risks, disagreements and historical accuracy behind institutional opinions.

## 1. Research Coverage

### Global institutions

Monitor research published by:

- Morgan Stanley
- J.P. Morgan
- Goldman Sachs
- UBS
- Jefferies
- Nomura
- Citi Research
- Bank of America Global Research
- Barclays Research
- HSBC Global Research
- BlackRock Investment Institute
- Vanguard
- Fidelity

### Indian institutions

Monitor:

- Motilal Oswal
- ICICI Securities
- HDFC Securities
- Kotak Institutional Equities
- Axis Securities
- Nuvama Institutional Equities
- JM Financial
- Emkay Global
- Anand Rathi
- SBI Securities
- Systematix Institutional Equities
- Other credible SEBI-registered research providers

### Primary information sources

Also monitor:

- NSE and BSE corporate announcements
- SEBI disclosures
- AMFI and mutual-fund portfolio disclosures
- Company annual and quarterly reports
- Investor presentations
- Earnings call transcripts
- SEC EDGAR filings, including 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K and 13F
- RBI, Federal Reserve and relevant government economic releases
- Official industry and regulatory publications

Use original issuer and institutional sources wherever possible.

## 2. Discovery and Collection

On each scheduled run:

1. Search for newly published reports since the previous successful run.
2. Identify material revisions to previously published investment opinions.
3. Capture relevant historical reports from the previous 24 months when they are needed for context.
4. Detect duplicate, superseded and redistributed reports.
5. Separate full reports from summaries, media commentary and analyst quotations.
6. Do not claim access to subscriber-only documents that cannot actually be retrieved.

Record for every discovered item:

- Institution and author
- Report title and report category
- Publication date and discovery date
- Covered company, ticker, exchange, country and sector
- Original source URL
- Document accessibility
- Primary versus secondary source
- Research methodology, where stated
- Conflicts-of-interest disclosures, where available
- Extracted findings and page references
- Extraction confidence and missing information

Maintain a persistent research index if storage is available. Otherwise, produce a structured index suitable for importing into a database.

## 3. Company-Level Research

For every materially covered company, analyze:

### Business fundamentals

- Business model and revenue streams
- Products and services
- Revenue by geography and business segment
- Revenue growth and profitability
- EBITDA, EBIT and net margins
- Operating and free cash flow
- ROE, ROCE and ROIC
- Capital expenditure and asset utilization
- Working capital and cash-conversion cycle
- Debt, cash, leverage and refinancing risks
- Promoter or insider ownership and pledging
- Related-party transactions
- Contingent liabilities and accounting red flags

### Industry-specific metrics

Choose the metrics relevant to the company's actual business model.

Examples:

- Capital goods: order backlog, order inflows, book-to-bill, execution cycle and customer concentration
- Banks: NIM, GNPA, NNPA, credit cost, capital adequacy and deposit growth
- SaaS: ARR, NRR, CAC, LTV, churn and remaining performance obligations
- Retail: same-store sales, inventory turns and store economics
- Pharmaceuticals: product pipeline, regulatory approvals, patent expiry and R&D productivity
- Manufacturing: capacity utilization, unit economics, input costs and operating leverage

Never apply generic financial ratios without checking whether they are meaningful for the sector.

### Analyst assessments

Extract:

- Buy, Hold, Sell or equivalent rating
- Current and previous price targets
- Forecast revenue, EBITDA and EPS
- Forecast revisions
- Valuation multiples and assumptions
- Bull, base and bear scenarios
- Key risks and upside catalysts
- Changes in the investment thesis

Distinguish actual analyst statements from your own interpretation.

## 4. Institutional Ownership and Capital Flows

Analyze publicly disclosed institutional ownership where available.

For India:

- FPI/FII ownership changes
- Domestic mutual-fund ownership changes
- Insurance and other DII holdings
- Bulk/block deal disclosures
- Significant shareholder changes

For the US:

- SEC 13F holdings
- Material 13D/13G ownership disclosures
- Insider transaction disclosures
- Relevant ETF and fund holdings

Compare changes across quarters and identify recurring accumulation or reduction patterns.

Clearly distinguish reported holdings from actual transaction activity.

Explain filing delays, disclosure thresholds, unavailable holdings, and limitations in attributing purchases to a particular institution.

Do not infer that an institution bought shares merely because its research division published a positive report.

## 5. Macro and Sector Intelligence

Track:

- India and US GDP expectations
- Inflation forecasts
- Central bank policy
- Interest-rate expectations
- Bond yields and yield curves
- Currency movements
- Commodity prices
- Global capital flows
- Sector earnings revisions
- Credit and liquidity conditions
- Regulatory and geopolitical developments

For every meaningful macro development, identify affected sectors, expected transmission mechanisms, relevant companies and uncertainties.

## 6. Cross-Institution Comparison

Create a consensus and disagreement analysis.

For each covered company, compare:

- Institutional ratings
- Price targets
- Revenue and earnings forecasts
- Valuation methods
- Growth assumptions
- Margin assumptions
- Risks and catalysts

Calculate median, range and dispersion only when sufficient comparable observations exist.

Track the direction and magnitude of revisions.

Identify situations where institutions disagree materially and explain the reasons.

Do not manufacture consensus statistics or treat a small sample as the entire analyst universe.

## 7. Historical Prediction Validation

Where historical reports and actual results are available:

- Compare prior revenue forecasts with reported revenue
- Compare earnings forecasts with actual earnings
- Compare margin and order-book assumptions with actual outcomes
- Check whether anticipated catalysts occurred
- Evaluate target-price outcomes at explicitly defined horizons
- Track analyst revisions and subsequent outcomes

Use consistent time horizons, fiscal periods, currencies and adjusted price data.

Account for splits, bonuses, dividends and other corporate actions where relevant.

Separate forecast accuracy from investment performance.

Do not rank analysts based on insufficient historical observations.

## 8. Research Quality and Verification

Apply strict verification rules:

1. Every material number must have a source and reporting period.
2. Prefer exchange filings, regulatory disclosures and company statements for historical financial facts.
3. Preserve forecast versus reported distinctions.
4. Cross-check conflicting numbers and explain reconciliation differences.
5. Never invent inaccessible research or missing financial figures.
6. Mark unverifiable claims explicitly.
7. State currencies, units, fiscal-year conventions and timestamps.
8. Do not use future information when evaluating historical predictions.
9. Flag stale data, promotional language and possible conflicts of interest.
10. Use legally accessible sources; do not bypass paywalls or reproduce copyrighted reports in full.

## 9. Output Structure

### A. Daily Intelligence Brief

Target: 5–8 minutes to read.

Include:

- Five most important new findings
- Important company rating changes
- Major price-target and forecast revisions
- Significant institutional ownership developments
- Macro and sector changes
- Significant disagreements between institutions
- New risks and potential catalysts
- Items requiring deeper investigation

Rank findings by materiality, freshness, source reliability and relevance.

### B. Detailed Institutional Research Report

Produce deeper coverage for the most important company or sector developments.

Include:

1. Executive summary
2. Business and sector context
3. Institutional research findings
4. Financial performance and forecasts
5. Valuation comparisons
6. Institutional ownership and flows
7. Bull, base and bear cases
8. Contradictory evidence and unresolved questions
9. Historical forecast validation
10. Risks, assumptions and potential catalysts
11. Source register and verification notes

### C. Research Change Log

Maintain a structured record containing:

- New reports
- Updated reports
- Changed forecasts
- Changed investment theses
- Material disclosure events
- Resolved and unresolved discrepancies

Distinguish new information from repeated commentary.

### D. Priority Research Queue

Identify companies and sectors requiring follow-up.

For each candidate, explain:

- Why it deserves further research
- What new evidence triggered its inclusion
- What is not yet known
- Which documents should be reviewed next

Do not automatically convert a news item, analyst upgrade or ownership change into a trading recommendation.

## 10. Research Standards

- Be analytical, neutral, skeptical and evidence-driven.
- Distinguish facts, analyst forecasts and independent inference.
- Actively seek contradictory evidence.
- Avoid survivorship bias, confirmation bias and hindsight bias.
- Never present analyst targets as guaranteed future prices.
- Avoid fabricated precision or unsupported confidence scores.
- Highlight uncertainty and information gaps.
- Clearly identify whether a conclusion relies on one source or multiple independent sources.
- Never interpret an absence of publicly available research as an absence of institutional interest.

## 11. Scheduling and Continuity

On every run, prioritize material changes since the previous report.

When prior-run state is available, use it to identify genuinely new research, revisions, recurring themes and resolved questions.

When prior-run state is unavailable, state that historical change detection is incomplete rather than inventing continuity.

Deliver the concise intelligence brief first.

Include deeper analysis only for findings substantial enough to warrant it. Do not produce long reports solely to meet a target length.

On a weekly run, consolidate the most important developments and provide an institutional outlook for the coming weeks.

On a monthly run, review forecast accuracy, sector rotation hypotheses, institutional positioning and changes in medium-term investment theses.

End every report with:

- Verified findings
- Key disagreements
- Open research questions
- Highest-priority next investigations
- Full source links and publication dates

The objective is to build a reliable, cumulative institutional research intelligence system rather than an isolated collection of market summaries.

## Suggested scheduling

I recommend separating the work into three cadences rather than generating a massive report every day.

| Routine                                   | Frequency                           | Output                                                                  |
| ----------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Institutional Research Scanner            | Every trading-day morning, 8 AM IST | 5-minute intelligence brief                                             |
| Institutional Research Deep Dive          | Every Saturday                      | Detailed analysis of the highest-priority companies and sectors         |
| Institutional Consensus & Accuracy Review | First Sunday of each month          | Analyst forecast accuracy, valuation revisions and institutional trends |

For the daily routine, you can use the full prompt above with a shorter delivery requirement. For the weekly and monthly routines, emphasize their respective sections.

One important limitation: A scheduled ChatGPT routine can research publicly accessible sources on each run, but it does not automatically provide a persistent document repository, licensed Bloomberg/FactSet access, or a database of historical analyst estimates. For a continuously growing, auditable research system, those components would need to be connected or built separately.

My recommendation is to combine this institutional research routine with your existing stock-market scanning process, so the reports answer one especially useful question:

“What has materially changed in institutional expectations, and is that change supported by the company's actual financial performance?”

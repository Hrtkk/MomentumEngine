# Goodluck India — Supply Chain & Customer Intelligence

Research Part 4 · Information available through 14 October 2026

This is an important addition to Goodluck's investment research. Its financial statements tell us how much revenue and profit it makes, but tracing its suppliers and customers helps us understand how defensible that revenue is, where the margins come from, and how exposed the company is to particular industries or counterparties.

The research has uncovered named steel suppliers, automotive customers, infrastructure companies, defence relationships and even identifiable US export transactions.

There is one critical distinction: a company appearing in Goodluck's annual report as a marquee client does not necessarily prove an active purchasing contract in FY26, or disclose how much revenue that customer generates.

### Dealer concentration

Goodluck's FY26 BRSR reports:

- Dealers and distributors accounted for 9.3% of total sales.
- Its top 10 dealers accounted for 47.2% of sales made through dealers.
- This implies the top 10 dealers represented approximately 4.4% of overall sales, assuming consistent denominators.

This suggests that concentration within the dealer channel is unlikely, by itself, to represent extreme group-wide customer concentration.

However, the top 10 direct OEM and project customers could have much greater importance. That information remains undisclosed in the evidence reviewed.&#x20;

## 7. How I would incorporate supply-chain tracing into every stock report

This deserves its own permanent research chapter with a relationship register.

Each customer or supplier should have a record containing the company name, relationship type, products exchanged, first and latest evidence dates, contract/order status, known transaction amounts, sources, and a confidence level.

I would use three evidence categories:

- Confirmed transaction: Public contract, purchase order, exchange disclosure or identifiable shipment.
- Company-disclosed relationship: A named client or supplier in investor presentations or annual reports, but with limited commercial details.
- Potential or historical relationship: Mentioned in older reports, inferred from industry connections, or awaiting current confirmation.

Most importantly, a customer logo should never become an assumed recurring order, and a supplier logo should never become an assumed percentage of raw-material purchases.

### What this adds to Goodluck's investment thesis

Goodluck appears to benefit from relationships across several large Indian manufacturing, automotive and infrastructure businesses, with predominantly domestic raw-material procurement.

That provides encouraging evidence of industrial reach. However, the economic quality of those relationships remains much less clear than their names.

The highest-value unanswered questions are whether Goodluck has pricing power with its major OEM customers, whether its steel sourcing is concentrated among a handful of mills, and how much business its largest direct customers contribute to revenue and receivables.

These should now sit alongside defence execution, ROCE and cash conversion as permanent Goodluck monitoring priorities.

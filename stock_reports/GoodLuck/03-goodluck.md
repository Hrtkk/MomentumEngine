### What would make Goodluck fundamentally stronger?

A sustained improvement in margins alongside rising ROCE and positive post-expansion free cash flow would be stronger evidence than revenue growth alone.

Its previously reported FY26 ROCE of roughly 14–15% is not yet consistent with a conclusively superior, capital-light manufacturing franchise. The higher-value product strategy needs to improve capital returns, not just the income statement.

## 3. The ₹285 crore defence fundraising — potential dilution

On 6 August 2026, Goodluck Defence and Aerospace approved a proposal to raise up to ₹285 crore by issuing shares to non-promoter investors at ₹375 per subsidiary share, subject to approvals.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.bazaarwatch.com\&sz=32)

BazaarWatch

+1



That implies up to 76 lakh new subsidiary shares if the entire amount is issued at the stated price.

But we cannot calculate the dilution percentage without the subsidiary's pre-issue capitalization.

Here's why this matters:

- The listed parent could continue consolidating 100% of subsidiary revenue while owning less than 100% of subsidiary equity.
- Outside investors could become entitled to part of the defence subsidiary's future profit.
- If a subsequent amalgamation involves issuing listed-parent shares, it could change Goodluck's per-share earnings and ownership structure.

The decisive missing information is whether the preferential allotment was completed and how the October amalgamation scheme would treat outside subsidiary shareholders.

Until those details are verified, we should not value Goodluck as though listed shareholders necessarily own 100% of future defence profits.

## 5. Governance audit — additional evidence

Goodluck's FY26 consolidated auditor expressed an unmodified opinion that the consolidated financial statements present a true and fair view under the applicable accounting framework.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://financialfilings.com\&sz=32)

FinancialFilings



This is positive, but an unmodified opinion does not mean all business risks are absent.

The annual report also documents intercompany financial transactions. We now have three connected research questions:

1. How much capital is the parent advancing to defence and other subsidiaries?
2. What economic interests will outside investors receive from the proposed subsidiary fundraising?
3. How will the planned amalgamation affect consolidated debt, minority interests, shareholder dilution and the parent company's financial statements?

The proposed transaction should be assessed through a before-and-after capitalization table, rather than treated automatically as earnings-accretive.

The balance of evidence supports a company with demonstrated historical earnings growth and credible expansion opportunities. It does not yet establish that current shareholders will earn sufficiently attractive returns from the capital being invested.

In particular, the proposed defence fundraising, amalgamation and working-capital requirements prevent a confident valuation conclusion.

What remains open: definitive October peer P/E/P/B/ROCE ranking, the completed subsidiary capitalization table and share-exchange terms, consolidated auditor annexures, and a properly adjusted technical price history. I have not presented those as completed.

For the next reporting cycle, I would make three variables central to the investment thesis: incremental ROCE, free cash flow after expansion capex, and defence earnings attributable per listed share. Those are the measurements most likely to reveal whether Goodluck is genuinely becoming a higher-quality manufacturer rather than simply becoming a larger one.

I can also monitor Goodluck's exchange filings for the final amalgamation scheme, contract award and quarterly results, and alert you when any of these materially changes the investment case.

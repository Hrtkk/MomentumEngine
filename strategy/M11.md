## Rule, in words

1. **Candidate list (weekly).** On the last session of each week, rank every eligible Liquid-500 name by its 6-month return `R6 = C/C[t−126] − 1`. Freeze the top 100 as this week's candidates.
2. **Trigger (any day).** A candidate is bought when, at the close:
   - the close is **above the highest high of the previous 252 sessions** (`hi252`, which excludes today), and
   - today's volume is **≥ 1.5 × the median volume of the previous 20 sessions**.
3. **Order.** Buy at the next session's open, 10% of book equity, whole shares. Candidates fill in rank order while slots (10) and the sector cap allow.
4. **Stop.** `close − 3 × ATR20`, fixed at entry, checked intraday every session from the entry session. A gap below the stop fills at the open.
5. **Exit (weekly).** At the weekly refresh, sell at the next open if the name's R6 rank has fallen **below 125**.
6. **Exit (monthly).** At the first session of a month, sell if the name left the Liquid-500 universe at the month-end reconstitution.
7. **Exit (data).** Sell after 5 consecutive sessions without an EQ/BE trade.

There is no profit target, no trailing stop and no indicator-based exit. Winners are held while they stay in the top 125 by 6-month return.

## Why a stock is picked — what the rule looks at

Only these facts, all from the official NSE close of the signal day:

| Fact | Printed in the signal record as |
|---|---|
| 6-month return and its rank among eligible names | `R6`, `rank` (must be ≤ 100) |
| Close vs the prior 252-session high | `vs_52w_high` (must be > 1.00) |
| Volume vs the 20-session median | `vol_x_median20` (must be ≥ 1.5) |
| ATR20 → stop distance | `atr20`, `stop` |

Everything else in the record (`R3`, `R12_1`, `rsi14`, `vs_sma20`, `day_ret`, `industry`, `nifty500_vs_200dma`) is recorded for the ledger and **does not influence the pick**. In particular, a high RSI or a stretched distance above SMA20 is *typical* of a name that just made a 52-week high: the rule buys strength on confirmation volume by design.

## Sizing and costs (common E2 rules)

10 slots · 10% of equity at entry · whole shares (so a ₹3,000+ stock gets 1 share ≈ 5%, and a stock above ₹6,000 cannot be bought) · max 3 per sector · cash allowed · no rebalancing · 0.6% round-trip cost. Since v1.1 (2026-10-08) the cap applies to known labels only, as SPEC_E2 §1 says; the E2 backtest ran with the older behaviour (cap also on the unlabelled group).

Holdout 90% bootstrap interval of annual excess: **[−0.6%, +24.4%]** (block 20, 5,000 replicates). Excess at 0.3 / 0.6 / 1.0% costs: +11.5 / +14.2 / +8.6%.

What this means in plain terms: about **two trades in three lose**; the return comes from the minority that keep running. Drawdowns of 30% have happened in both periods. A string of losing entries is the normal state of this rule, not a sign that it has broken.

## What it does not do

- It does not judge "momentum strength" from indicators (MACD, RSI, Bollinger width, volume trend). It only asks for a new 252-session high on ≥ 1.5× volume. Tier-4 of E2 tested an **extension veto** on the 3-month rule (M9: skip buys if day return > 5%, close > 1.10 × SMA20 or RSI14 > 75). M9 failed development (positive in only 2 of 4 blocks) and in the holdout earned **+13.7% excess vs +20.9% for the same rule without the veto** (M1). On this data the veto removed return; it did not add safety.
- It cannot buy stocks priced above the slot (₹6,000 at ₹60k capital).
- The sector label comes from today's Nifty 500 list; most Liquid-500 mid/small caps have no label.
- No market-regime filter. M7 (market filter) failed development.

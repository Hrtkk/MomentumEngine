## Rule, in words

1. **Candidates (weekly).** Rank eligible names by **M5 = mean of percentile(R6 / σ126) and percentile(R12 / σ252)** — volatility-adjusted 6- and 12-month momentum, NSE Momentum-30 style. Freeze the top 50.
2. **Event (day T0).** A candidate prints a **day return ≥ +5% on volume ≥ 2 × the 20-session median**, closing at or above the midpoint of its own range (v1.1). One active event per name; a name already held cannot start one.
3. **Watch (T0+1 … T0+7).** The event is cancelled if the close falls below the **midpoint of the T0 bar**, the name drops out of the top 50, or 7 sessions pass.
4. **Confirmation (earliest T0+3).** Buy at the next open when the close is **≥ the T0 high, above every high since T0, and the average volume since T0 is below the T0 volume** (a quiet pull-back / consolidation that then resolves upward). First confirmation only; the event is consumed either way.
5. **Stop.** `close − 3 × ATR20`, fixed at entry, intraday.
6. **Exit (daily).** Close below the **T0 low** → sell next open.
7. **Exit (weekly).** M5 rank > 125 at a refresh → sell. Monthly universe exit and 5-session no-trade exit as in the other books.

## Why a stock is picked

| Fact | Where it shows |
|---|---|
| Volatility-adjusted momentum rank ≤ 50 | `rank` |
| A ≥ 5% up-day on ≥ 2× volume within the last 7 sessions | event T0 (date, high, low, volume) |
| Close back above the T0 high with lighter volume in between | confirmation day = signal day |
| T0 low | `t0low` in the position record — the exit level |

This is the only one of the three books with a **timing element**: it waits for the spike to settle and only buys if the name then takes out the spike high. It is the rule closest to the owner's concern about buying exhausted moves.

## What it does not do

- It does not use RSI, MACD or Bollinger width. Its "not exhausted" test is structural: close holds above the spike midpoint, then clears the spike high on lighter interim volume.
- It can sit in cash for weeks (no events, or events that fail to confirm). That is expected, not a malfunction.

# Research R1 — Why does M11 work? (2026-10-07, development data only)

**Question:** Which features of M11's 52-week-high breakout signals are associated with forward excess return vs B2-net: volume ratio, distance above the prior 52W high, first vs repeat breakout?
**Method:** All M11-style breakouts on signal dates 2013-01-01..2020-12-31 (frozen R6 top-100, C > prior-252 high, eligible, valid ATR stop), *without* the 1.5× volume filter so rejected volumes can be compared. Enter open t+1, exit close t+h, 0.6% round trip, minus B2-net compounded over t+1..t+h. Forward windows must end ≤ 2020-12-31. Means with 90% month-cluster bootstrap CIs. Book-independent (no slots/sector cap/exits).
**Result:** 8,722 breakouts, 6,722 M11 signals (773 names, 1,661 dates). M11 signals: excess −0.95% (5d), −0.54% (10d), +0.24% (20d), **+4.05% [+2.7,+5.4] (60d)**; but medians are −1.6% (5–20d) and +0.36% (60d), hit rate 49% at 60d, and **dropping the top 5% of signals takes the 60d mean to ≈0.00%**. Breakouts the volume filter rejects: 60d +2.84% [+1.0,+4.8] — overlapping CI, no monotone volume pattern (<1× bucket +4.16%, ≥3× +4.48%). Distance: 3–5% above the high is best at 20d/60d (+1.47%/+5.92%); >5% weakest short-term. First vs repeat: no material difference at 60d under either definition.
**Caveats:** Overlapping 60-session windows and many signals per date (median 3, max 36) — effective N is far below 6,722; 5 grouping cuts × 4 horizons ≈ 60+ comparisons, so single-bucket "wins" are expected by chance; signal-level ≠ book-level (the book holds until R6 rank > 125 and is capped at 10 slots); bucket edges for first/repeat were chosen by me (63 sessions).
**Next:** Hypothesis only (not a rule change): M11's development edge looks like a right-tail capture at ~3-month horizons, not a high-hit-rate signal, and the 1.5× volume filter is not shown to add value. R2 next.

## Definitions and choices (made before looking at results)
- Breakout set = `Breakout52` candidate rule minus the volume test: R6 rank ≤ 100 from the latest ISO-week-end refresh ≤ t (`P.rank_of(P.R6, ·)`), `C_t > hi252_t` (prior 252-session max of **High**), `eligible_t`, `ok_atr`. M11 signal = additionally `V_t ≥ 1.5·medV20_t`. Positions in the book are ignored, so a stock can signal repeatedly.
- Volume ratio = `V_t / medV20_t` (medV20 is the prior 20-session median). Distance = `C_t / hi252_t − 1`.
- **Repeat A:** the stock had an M11 signal (full rule) in the previous 63 sessions. **Repeat B:** any close above its prior-252 high in the previous 63 sessions (price only). Both computed on signals within the dev period (repeat A's memory starts 2013-01-01).
- Forward return: `C_{t+h} / O_{t+1} × (1−0.003)/(1+0.003) − 1`. Missing closes after a stock stops trading are forward-filled (last close held) — this treats a delisting as flat, which can flatter losers; not quantified.
- Benchmark: `run_e1.b2_series(P, start_i)` net leg, log-compounded from session t+1 to t+h. B2's day t+1 return includes the overnight gap close t → open t+1 (with old weights), which the stock leg does not; this is a small mismatch and is the same across buckets.
- Costs: 0.6% is subtracted from every stock leg, so 5-day figures start ~−0.6% behind; comparisons between buckets are unaffected.
- CI: 2,000 resamples of calendar months (seed 20261007), 90% percentile interval. This clusters same-month signals but not overlap across adjacent months, so the intervals are too narrow, especially at 60d.

## Reading (facts, not recommendations)
- **Horizon:** at the signal level the excess is negative for 1–2 weeks after entry (partly the 0.6% cost) and only turns clearly positive by 60 sessions. All 8 dev years have a positive 60d mean (2018: +0.19%, CI includes 0); 2020's 60d hit rate is 38% with a +6.0% mean — the most tail-driven year.
- **Volume:** no monotone relation. The rejected <1.5× breakouts have 60d excess +2.84% vs +4.05% for M11 signals; CIs overlap. At 5d/10d the rejected group is slightly *better*. The data here do not show the volume filter as the source of M11's edge.
- **Distance above the high:** 3–5% leads at 10/20/60d; >5% is weakest at 5/10d and has the lowest 60d hit rate (44%). With ~16 comparisons in this table alone, the 3–5% lead may be noise.
- **First vs repeat:** repeat (A) is slightly better at 10–60d; under definition B first-time breakouts are rare (630) and similar at 60d. No clear effect.
- **Tail dependence:** the 60d mean is carried by the best ~5% of signals. A strategy holding 10 of ~3 daily candidates samples this tail randomly; book outcomes will be lumpy.

## Codex review (2026-10-08)

- **Timing / period:** Recomputed signals and all four return columns match the CSV; every measured exit is ≤2020-12-31. Shifted highs/volume, contemporaneous ranks and next-open entry show no direct signal look-ahead. However, `pit_panels.build()` loads a through-2026 cache, retrospective symbol mappings and corporate-action adjustments; “No 2021–26 data is used” is too absolute. Future uniform split scaling alone does not imply leakage. No truncated-source invariance check was performed. Per `ROADMAP.md`, 2021–26 is already spent, never fresh validation for R1-derived changes.
- **Hit-rate error (`table`):** `(g[x] > 0).mean()` counts missing outcomes as losses. Among observed M11 outcomes, 60d hit rate is **3276/6460 = 50.7%**, not 49%; 2020 is **403/792 = 50.9%**, not 38%. Thus the highlighted 2020 low-hit-rate claim is false. Compute hits after `dropna()`. Headline 773 names / 1,661 dates describes all breakouts; M11 has **760 / 1,571**.
- **Data / execution (`forward`):** 23 observed M11 60d outcomes use missing terminal closes carried forward; 13 assume next-open purchases rejected by `sim.fillable` (mean excess +32.6%). These are hypothetical marks, not executable returns. Quantify stale-price, corporate-action-neutralisation and fillability sensitivity before attributing an edge.
- **Benchmark:** B2 includes the entry-day overnight return; the stock does not. That exposure differs across signal dates/buckets: mean initial B2 overnight is +0.296% for M11 versus +0.279% for rejected signals with 60d outcomes. Align entry timestamps; “small” and “same across buckets” are not established by the method.
- **Inference (`boot_ci`):** Independent entry-month resampling misses cross-month 60-session overlap and persistent same-name dependence. Use joint calendar blocks covering the overlap, with longer-block and issuer/episode sensitivity. Separately overlapping CIs do not test bucket differences or establish equivalence. There are **64 bucket/horizon means, 96 including years**, plus prior strategy selection; intervals are pointwise, not multiplicity-adjusted. “Clearly positive”, “best” and “no material difference” must remain descriptive pending dependence-aware contrast tests and multiplicity control.
- **Interpretation:** The +4.05% mean, +0.36% median and ≈0% top-5%-trimmed mean reproduce. Repeated signals can count the same winning episode many times; this is not trade-level attribution. The book selects by rank, capacity and sector constraints, not randomly, so the “samples this tail randomly” assertion is unsupported.

REVIEW ISSUES: missing-as-loss hit rates; mislabeled counts; stale/unfillable returns; benchmark timing mismatch; incomplete dependence and multiplicity treatment; unsupported book attribution; overly absolute data-isolation claim.

**BLOCKING findings and exact fixes**

1. **A9 is outcome-dependent accounting, and the corporate-action parser loses distinct actions with equal factors.** `pit_panels.py:137–146` accepts/selects an adjustment using the ex-day closing price. Lines 162–172 set the factor to the reciprocal of the observed closing ratio, forcing the adjusted close-to-close return to zero. The factor also changes ex-day opening exposure and whether a pre-existing stop is crossed, even though that day's close was unavailable at its open. Correct, fixed split factors applied consistently to historical prices, volume, quantities, and stops do not by themselves create predictive look-ahead; choosing those factors from the future close does.

   Separately, `ca_factors()` at lines 65–68 applies `set()` to numerical factors. A separately filed 2× split and 2× bonus become `[2]`, losing the required 4× product. Both 360ONE on 2023-03-02 and CGCL on 2024-03-05 demonstrate this. The fallback zeroed genuine split-adjusted returns of −0.5132% and +20.0021%, respectively. The PHILIPCARB manual override similarly zeroed +4.9845% instead of applying its 5× split. See the five-event table below.

   There are 78 neutralisations in both the cache and saved books: 77 feed-based events plus the manual override. Thus the spec's 77 plus one manual event is reconcilable. They are not economically proven neutral events. Demerger consideration, rights subscription costs/entitlements, special dividends, and genuine price discovery cannot be reconstructed by declaring the observed return zero. Bias direction in strategy excess is not established: this can erase gains as well as losses and affects the benchmark and strategies differently. It also changes later momentum, volatility, ATR, volume comparisons, and eligibility alerts. M11 held JSL across the 2015-11-19 neutralisation; M5 held STAR across the 2024-12-06 neutralisation. Absence of direct exposure to other neutralisations does not remove their later signal effects.

   **Exact fix:** deduplicate action records by action identity/type and authoritative terms, not factor value; multiply distinct simultaneous actions, retaining 2×2=4. Derive factors from dated exchange/company entitlements, with price ratios serving only as validation alerts. Use a security-level entitlement ledger for cash, successor shares, rights, and demerger assets, with specified valuation and availability dates. Do not select factors or erase returns from ex-day closes. Verify and apply PHILIPCARB's 5× split. Unresolved material actions must make the affected comparison unresolved, with exposure and valuation bounds disclosed; do not retrospectively delete the security or its loss. Rebuild panels and rerun development selection and all comparisons after correction.

2. **B2-net is not a self-financing executable Liquid-500 benchmark.** `run_e2.py:21,75` imports `run_e1.b2_series`; the defects are at `run_e1.py:24–38`.

   - It resets overnight weights to `W.shift(2)`, ignoring the preceding session's intraday price drift. An actual portfolio carries its previous close holdings into the next opening gap. Daily reset to the old target at the preceding close is an implicit uncharged trade.
   - Turnover is consequently measured against the wrong opening inventory. `gross - cost/2*turn` does not actually fund trades and their fees at the open; fees should also affect subsequent intraday exposure. Setting the first day's return to zero does not establish a cash-start portfolio or charge the initial investment.
   - Targets come from daily `P.eligible`, not just Liquid-500 membership. The holdout universe has 500 members throughout, but eligible targets average 498.737 and reach a minimum of 474. BE/BZ status, missing rows, and the 253-session alert exclusion silently change this benchmark's constituents.
   - Missing overnight/intraday observations become zero returns, and target changes assume fills without `sim.fillable` or pending orders. I found 106 decreases in target weight on locked-down days, 55 increases on locked-up days, and 63 positive target observations with missing opens after the test start. These are concrete violations of the claimed common execution model; counts concern target changes, not a reconstructed actual ledger.

   An independent row-by-row implementation reproduces saved B2-net to a maximum daily error of 2.23e−16. A diagnostic that carries previous close weights and funds fees, while deliberately retaining the same targets and permissive/missing-price assumptions, changes holdout CAGR from 21.1530% to 20.3275% and MDD from 24.5394% to 24.8392%; development CAGR changes from 12.0588% to 11.1919%. This is an isolated sensitivity, **not** a validated replacement benchmark.

   **Exact fix:** maintain independent cash, quantities, last marks, and pending orders for B2-gross and B2-net. Register whether equal weights refresh daily (inherited E1 convention) or monthly, and which series may be bought versus retained. Form targets from information available at close t, carry actual old holdings through the next opening gap, execute sells and affordable buys at t+1 with the common fill rules, charge actual traded notional, and mark remaining holdings through close. Include initial purchase fees. Do not create cash or remove unfilled holdings through weight renormalisation. Report membership/execution exclusions separately and apply the corrected hurdle to both dev selection and holdout verdicts.

3. **Security identity and terminal corporate events are incomplete; delisted/merged holdings can remain permanently alive at stale marks.** `pit_panels.py:16–31,73–85` relies on an incomplete current symbol-change list and ignores effective dates/ISIN continuity when mapping symbols. The cached raw rows show PHILIPCARB on 2022-01-12 and PCBL on 2022-01-13 have the identical ISIN `INE602A01023`, with ₹247.05 carried into the new row's previous close. They nevertheless occupy separate panel columns: PHILIPCARB ends on January 12 and PCBL begins on January 13. This breaks lookbacks and listing-age/universe eligibility and can strand positions.

   `e2_strategies.py:16–19` requests an exit after five missing sessions, but `sim.py:321–329,369–375` cannot execute it without a future quote and continues marking at the last close indefinitely. Independent M11 reconstruction ends with ESSAROIL (last quote 2016-02-09, 64.690765 shares × ₹262.90) and GSKCONS (last quote 2020-04-15, 1.975689 shares × ₹10,732.60) still occupying two of ten slots. Combined stale value is ₹38,211.48. GSKCONS was a share-exchange merger, not an asset that remained frozen: the issuer's terms specify 4.39 HUL shares for each GSKCONS share. [HUL annual report](https://www.hul.co.in/files/92ui5egz/production/deffeb1a406d0dda5445caa3d5731b849a137bf1.pdf)

   **Exact fix:** introduce permanent security identities with effective-dated ticker/ISIN histories and explicit split/merger/conversion links. Reconcile overlapping aliases instead of silently choosing one duplicate row. Stitch verified renames without resetting age. Apply actual delisting proceeds, successor share quantities, and cancellation/recovery events on documented dates; retain an explicit unresolved valuation state while information is unavailable. Count and publish stale exposures and unresolved orders in saved books/results, and block conclusive verdicts while material positions remain unresolved. A requested `NO_TRADE_5` exit is not a completed liquidation. Including historical delisted names is a substantial improvement over E1, but this lifecycle does not justify “no survivorship or inclusion bias.”

4. **The exchange calendar is incomplete, and unsuccessful downloads can silently become holidays.** `fetch_pit.py:84` and `:138` exclude every Saturday/Sunday. The cache has no weekend sessions. NSE explicitly held equity sessions on 2024-01-20 and 2024-05-18; both are absent. This changes session-count lookbacks, event windows, weekly refresh dates, next-session execution, and potentially stops. [January settlement/trading notice](https://nsdl.co.in/downloadables/pdf/2024-0009-Policy-Settlement_Holiday_on_January_22%2C_2024.pdf); [NSE May 18 circular](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/circulars/MSD61893.pdf)

   `_get()` returns the same `None` for unavailable/error cases and holidays; `bhav()` drops those results and skips existing prior-year files. `update()` starts after the latest cached date, so an earlier internal gap remains unrepaired. Date verification checks only the first row, although A11 correctly adds the two-digit-year parsing fallback. Checking a retrieved file's date does not prove every session was retrieved.

   **Exact fix:** reconcile every expected session against an authoritative exchange calendar, including special sessions. Persist retrieval status and distinguish holidays, download failures, malformed files, and verified empty data. Retry/backfill all missing sessions, including inside existing yearly files; validate all distinct internal dates and basic OHLC/volume fields. Refuse a final backtest with unresolved calendar holes. Recompute indicators and genuine week/month ends from the repaired calendar.

5. **The sector cap violates the spec and introduces a present-day coverage preference.** SPEC_E2 §1 says the three-position cap applies only to known sectors. `sim.py:336` instead groups every symbol absent from today's industry series under the same `"?"` label and caps that group at three. Of 1,490 panel symbols, 1,018 lack that current label. This disproportionately restricts historical/delisted names while today's covered names can use multiple industry groups.

   In an independent M11 replay, this rule rejected 600 buy attempts because the unknown group was full. Changing only the replay's cap to exempt unknown labels changes holdout CAGR from 35.3331% to 29.9743%, MDD from 30.5100% to 31.5673%, and total closed trades from 447 to 464. It still passes the saved hurdle's mechanical rule, so this sensitivity alone does not demonstrate a verdict reversal; it demonstrates a material spec deviation and a +5.36 pp CAGR advantage from the implemented cap in this comparison.

   **Exact fix:** normalise absent/NaN/Unknown labels, and enforce the industry cap only when the candidate has a known label; compare only known, equal labels. Rerun every strategy and development selection. For a genuinely historical industry policy, use dated classifications; otherwise label current-sector usage as a disclosed non-PIT approximation and show a sector-cap sensitivity.

6. **An untouched, one-shot historical holdout is not established.** The current file order supports selection-before-holdout-table, and the selected set is mechanically correct. However, E1's `results/metrics.csv` already existed at 16:31:51 UTC on October 7, before SPEC_E2's creation at 16:45:43 UTC, and contains annual results for 2022–2026. `SPEC.md` also expressly records prior inspection of 2026-09-04 through 2026-10-07. These overlap E2's declared 2021–2026 holdout. File existence does not prove exactly what a person examined, but the earlier experiment and acknowledged inspection prevent certification of an untouched test period.

   In addition, the dev command simulates and saves the complete holdout books before selection (`run_e2.py:37–42,71–86`), and `holdout()` only requires that a mutable selection file exist. It can be repeated or run after overwrites. Dev trade win/holding-period statistics use the eventual exit of trades entered during dev (`:63–67`), potentially after dev; dev alert counts cover the entire simulated window (`:42,105`). Those fields do **not** enter selection, but the development output is not fully isolated from future outcomes.

   **Exact fix:** retain E2 as historical exploratory screening, withdraw “untouched/tested once” as an established claim, and reserve confirmation for a newly registered prospective interval. Freeze code, data/calendar/action manifests, strategy parameters, and selection hashes; make the dev job stop at the dev boundary and save its full continuation state. Only after sealing selection should a separate job continue into validation. Prevent silent overwrite/repeated evaluation and preserve an append-only run log. Do not relabel another already examined historical slice as fresh holdout. The reported bootstrap cannot repair prior inspection or these data/model defects.

7. **Monthly membership becomes actionable one session late.** `pit_panels.py:119–125` installs a month-end selection only on rows after the month-end. `E2Mixin.daily_exits()` at lines 14–23 detects the change at the first new-month close and submits an exit for the second new-month open. Month-end ranks also use the old membership. This is not future leakage; it is a departure from a reconstitution decided at month-end and executed at the next open. Missing prices on the first new-month day additionally bypass the membership-exit branch.

   **Exact fix:** keep distinct “membership known for next open” and “membership currently held” states. Compute the new set at the month-end close, use it for reconstitution decisions and the next opening targets, and retry unavailable exits without losing the reconstitution instruction. Register whether a coincident weekly/monthly rank refresh uses the new set. Verify the same convention in strategies and B2, then rerun.

8. **M10 does not enforce the required T0 midpoint condition when creating an event.** `sim.S7Confirm.decide()` checks active events at lines 157–162, then creates new ones at lines 169–172 without checking `C_T0 >= (H_T0+L_T0)/2`. An event failing on T0 can survive if it recovers on T0+1 and later confirm. This contradicts inherited A6, which explicitly includes T0. `EventConfirm` inherits this logic unchanged.

   **Exact fix:** require the T0 midpoint condition before adding an event (or immediately invalidate the new event on that same close); maintain that invariant through confirmation and expiry. **Non-blocking findings, qualifications, and reporting repairs**

- **What the universe gets right:** the ₹50 filter uses nominal contemporaneous close and liquidity uses raw traded value, avoiding the earlier E1 future-split absolute-price-threshold defect (`pit_panels.py:113–125`). Universe construction starts from historical EQ/BE/BZ records and retains names selected in any historical month; it does not filter to today's index constituents. Keeping the union of historical selections for storage is not itself a future-membership signal. A10 excludes `INF` ISINs. However, security identity, lifecycle, sector coverage, and calendar defects above preclude the categorical “no survivorship or inclusion bias” statement.
- **Independent universe samples:** rebuilding the cross-section from all cached raw securities, applying the current alias mapping, INF exclusion, age/nominal-price tests and 63-row liquidity rule, gave exactly the cached following-month 500-name sets for month ends **2012-12-31, 2017-12-29, 2020-12-31, 2023-12-29, and 2026-09-30**. Each symmetric difference was empty. This verifies the implemented ranking against the cache, conditional on its incomplete calendar and alias map; it does not establish raw-source completeness.
- **Numerical specification:** the liquidity median uses 63 calendar-panel rows with only 40 observations required, while the prose says “previous 63 sessions”; listing age counts nonmissing historical close rows, not elapsed exchange sessions. Register explicitly whether month-end itself is included, how suspensions affect the count, and how liquidity ties are resolved. The current top-500 sort lacks an explicit symbol tie-break. These are not evidence of forward-looking inputs but require a precise convention.
- **Opening-fill hindsight:** `sim.fillable()` uses execution-day high, low, close and total volume to accept/reject an opening fill. The inherited spec explicitly calls for the H=L lock proxy, so this is a prescribed daily-bar simulation assumption rather than an undisclosed strategy look-ahead. Nevertheless those end-of-day facts are unavailable at the opening order.
